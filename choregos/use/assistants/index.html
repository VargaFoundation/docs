Assistants

An assistant is an agent you talk to. Your organisation installs it for a group of people, and you find it in the console. It answers from the sources it was given, and says where each answer comes from. At acme, the billing team gives its people a billing assistant that answers questions about invoices.

What you will be able to do

  • Find the assistants you may talk to, and start a conversation.
  • Read the citations under an answer, and rate it.
  • Confirm or refuse a write the assistant proposes.
  • See, correct, add to and export what the assistant remembers about you.
  • Connect your own accounts, so the assistant reads your data and nobody else's.
  • Subscribe to a task, and decide whether it may read for you while you are away.

Before you start​

  • You belong to one of the audience groups the assistant was installed for. If you do not, the assistant is not in your list. Ask an organisation administrator.
  • Nothing else. You do not need a project role.

Find an assistant and talk to it​

  1. Open Assistants in the sidebar. Each card shows the assistant's name, what it does and a badge: ready, incomplete or suspended.
  2. Press Converse on a ready assistant. The conversation opens in your personal space: only you read it.
  3. Type in Message and press Send. The answer streams in as it is written. Press Cancel to stop a turn; what the assistant already did stays in the journal.
  4. To come back later, open My conversations (the page is titled Conversations). Each line shows the assistant, the number of turns, the cost and the last activity. Open one to continue it, or press Delete to remove it.

A conversation left alone for a while goes idle. Your next message reopens it with its history.

What you should see: the answer, then under it a badge for each document the assistant used, written as the document's title and version.

Citations​

The assistant is asked to cite what it uses from its knowledge. The badges under an answer name the documents. A figure that comes from a source should carry its date. If an answer has neither, ask the assistant where it comes from, and treat it as unchecked.

Thumbs and feedback​

Under the last answer, press the thumb up (Good answer) or the thumb down (Bad answer). A text box, A comment (optional), opens; press Send feedback. A thumb down can become a test case for the people who maintain the assistant, but it only does so after one of them has read it and decided. Your comment does not enter a test on its own.

Confirm a write​

An assistant never writes to a source by itself. When it wants to, a card The assistant wants to act appears, with the operation, a summary, the arguments, and, when the source can tell, What would change.

  1. Read the card.
  2. Press Confirm to let it go ahead, or Refuse.

Only you, the person who owns the conversation, can decide. The write is made with your identity, and the source decides whether you are allowed to make it. A proposal that nobody decides within 24 hours expires.

What the assistant remembers​

From Assistants, press Memory, accounts and tasks on a card. The Memory tab shows one card per memory space the assistant keeps about you.

  • A fact the assistant proposes to remember shows a proposed badge. Press Confirm to keep it or Reject to drop it. The same card also appears in the conversation: The assistant proposes to remember.
  • Pin keeps a fact in front of the assistant. Unpin undoes it. Delete removes one fact.
  • Add a fact (or Add a profile (JSON) for a profile space) then Add writes something yourself. It is active at once. For a space kept per subject, name the subject in Subject key first.
  • Export downloads one space as a file. Export everything at the top downloads all of them.
  • Forget all empties one space, after a confirmation.
  • Erase everything about me, under the cards, removes every conversation and every remembered fact this assistant holds about you, after a confirmation. This cannot be undone.

Each card says who may write to the space, under its name (writes: agent_proposes, for example, means the assistant proposes and you decide).

Connected accounts​

Some sources belong to you: your bank, your mailbox, your brokerage. The assistant reads them only with an account you connected.

  1. Open the Connected accounts tab, or the answer's warning An account is not connected with its Connect your (source name) account button.
  2. You are sent to the source's own page to give your consent. You come back to the console.
  3. The account then shows Connected, the scopes you gave and when it was last used.

Revoke removes the connection at once, and Choregos forgets its secret. Reconnect appears when a source stopped accepting the account. Until you connect, the assistant carries on without that source and says so; it never reads it with someone else's rights. The same list is at /me/connected-accounts.

Tasks, and the standing delegation​

Besides conversations, an assistant may offer tasks: work it does for you in the background, like a morning brief. The Tasks tab lists them. The Inbox tab holds what they delivered, and your organisation may also send them by email.

  1. Choose how it runs: On demand, or On a schedule with a cron and your Time zone.
  2. Press Subscribe. Run now starts it once, and the result lands in your inbox.
  3. Pause, Resume and Unsubscribe stop it as you wish.

When a task reads for you while you are away​

A task that reads your accounts when you are not there needs a standing delegation: your agreement, for that task only.

  1. Under the task, press Review and agree. The console lists the exact sources and operations it would read.
  2. Press I agree to these reads.

A delegation is bounded:

  • it reads only, never writes;
  • it ends after 90 days unless your installation sets less, and you are reminded before; press Agree again to renew it;
  • Revoke the delegation ends it at once;
  • it is suspended on its own if you leave the audience or if the plugin changes what the task reads: you agree again.
Your organisation may not offer delegated tasks yet

The standing delegation is delivered, but its decision record (ADR 0137) is still proposed. An organisation should not offer delegated tasks until its works council has given its opinion and its data protection impact assessment is done. If you see no Review and agree button, or your administrator tells you the feature is off, that is why. Tasks that read only public sources, and tasks you run yourself, do not need a delegation.
