Decisions

A workflow names the points where a person decides. Choregos never guesses past them: the work item waits, with a deadline, until someone with the right role and group decides. This page shows where to find what waits for you and how to answer each kind of request.

What you will be able to do

  • Find everything that waits for a person, across your projects.
  • Approve or send back, answer a question, complete a task.
  • Approve a governed action and a production release.
  • Know who may decide what, and what happens when nobody does.

Before you start​

  • To decide a step of a work item: Developer or above on the project, and membership of the group the step is addressed to (for example maintainers). Groups come from your identity provider, as of your last sign-in.
  • To approve a production release: Release captain.
  • To approve a governed action: the role and group its approval rule names, and a recent sign-in.

Find what waits for you​

Open Inbox in the sidebar. Its count is the number of things waiting for a person. It has four parts:

CardWhat waits
TicketsWork items stopped on a person: an approval, a question, a change of scope, a task. Overdue first, then the nearest deadline
Actions to approveGoverned actions: writes into other systems, waiting for their approvers
Network accessRequests to open a host for agents, waiting for an organisation admin
Budgets crossedBudgets that reached their alert threshold today

Inside a project, its own Inbox shows the same for that project only. You can decide a work item from the inbox, or open it and use its Decision awaited card.

Approve or send back​

  1. Open the work item. Read what the step produced in Artifacts: the specification, the plan, the diff.
  2. To accept it, press Approve.
  3. To refuse it, type what is wrong in Reason for sending back and press Send back. The workflow goes where it says on a rejection, usually back to the agent, which receives your reason.

From the command line:

choregos items approve <item-id>
choregos items reject <item-id> --reason "The export must include credit notes."

Answer a question​

When an agent is blocked, it does not guess: it asks. The work item moves to a waiting state (often Needs a human) and the people of the step's group are notified.

  1. Open the work item. The question is in Decision awaited.
  2. Type your reply in Answer and press Answer.

The agent's stage resumes where it stopped, with your answer and one more attempt.

choregos items answer <item-id> "Use the customer's billing currency."

Accept or refuse a change of scope​

An agent that needs to touch a path outside its allowed scope asks for it. A small request (a few files, as the project's policy allows) is granted on its own. A larger one waits for a person: press Approve to widen the scope for this work item, or Send back with a reason.

Complete a task​

Some steps are done by a person in the real world: hand over a badge, set up a workstation, sign a contract. The step shows a form and, often, a sentence to attest.

  1. Open the work item. The task's instructions are in Decision awaited.
  2. Fill in the form. Required fields are listed under "To fill" until they are filled.
  3. If the task asks for it, tick I attest and the sentence it shows.
  4. Press Done. The values become fields of the work item, and the next step reads them.

If the task cannot be done, write why in Why it cannot be done and press Cannot do it.

Approve a governed action​

A write into a system that is not Choregos (create an account, order a laptop, deactivate a badge) is a governed action. An agent or a workflow proposes it; the people its approval rule names decide; then the platform runs it, step by step, and undoes what it did if a step fails.

  1. Open Inbox, then the action under Actions to approve. Its page lists what it will do, effect by effect, and with which parameters.
  2. Press Approve, or write a Reason to reject and press Reject.

Three rules apply:

  • A recent sign-in. An approval rule may require that you signed in a few minutes ago. If you did not, the console sends you through your identity provider again, then brings you back.
  • Not your own proposal. The person who proposed the action, or who owns the agent that proposed it, does not decide it.
  • Several approvers. A rule may ask for two different people.

The action's page then shows its journal: every effect, its attempts, its answer or its error.

Approve a production release​

A release train batches what is ready and deploys it. When the project's policy or the workflow asks for an approval before production:

  1. Open the project's Trains.
  2. Find the batch waiting for approval and press Approve. The confirmation names the batch and how many work items it carries.
choregos trains status billing-api
choregos trains approve <release-id> --note "Checked with support"

Deadlines and escalations​

Every human step can carry a deadline (sla_hours on the group, timeout_hours on the transition). The inbox puts overdue requests first. When a step times out, the work item goes where its workflow says, usually Needs a human, and from there a person decides to resume or abandon it.

Who decides when nobody can​

A step addressed to a group that nobody carries would wait forever. An organisation admin may answer any step; doing so outside the group is recorded in the audit log as an override, with the group it bypassed.

Decisions stay in the console​

A /choregos approve, reject or answer comment on an issue does not decide anything. Choregos replies on the issue with a link to the inbox. A decision is taken by a signed-in person, in the console or with their own token.

What can go wrong​

MessageWhat it means
"deciding needs at least the developer role"You are a viewer on this project
"this step is addressed to the group … only its members answer it"You do not carry the step's group at your last sign-in; sign in again after your identity provider adds you, or ask an organisation admin
"no human request is pending on this work item"Someone decided before you, or the work item moved on
"approving a release needs the release_captain role"Ask a project owner for the role
